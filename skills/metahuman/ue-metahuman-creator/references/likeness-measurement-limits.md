# Likeness measurement limits (face fitting to AI references)

Lessons from fitting a MetaHuman to a six-view AI-generated head turnaround.

- **Landmark error can't see volume.** IPD-normalised MediaPipe landmark error measures only where features sit. Cheek mass, jaw definition, brow mass, ear shape and nose form (tip roundness, wing flare, nostril show) never register, and neither do nasion-aligned profile numbers for those, so a "metric at floor" or "nose within 0.1 cm" result can coexist with a user who clearly sees no likeness. When the user disagrees with the numbers, stop sculpting from numbers: ask for a markup with directions, or for a single feature show donor-preset crops and let the user pick.
- **Check the references against each other first.** Compare 3/4-left against a mirrored 3/4-right. AI turnaround views were each edited independently, so they disagree by about as much as a good fit differs from them. Pick hero views (front + one profile) instead of chasing parity with all of them.
- **A generated 3D head is a shape target only on the user's eye, never on its score.** Multi-image Meshy heads scored worse on the landmark metric than the template-fitted MetaHuman against the same references:
  - first run with hair, front 2.3 vs the fitted MetaHuman's 1.0;
  - hairless inputs, `image_enhancement:false`, front + profile only: front 1.9.
  
  That metric cannot see form (first bullet), so a poor score does not disqualify a head the user has looked at and approved as the character; such a head becomes the conform target (face-fitting reference, "User-approved generated head as the conform target"). For a head the user has not approved, use it for skull, ear and hair outline at most and validate any gap derived from it against the reference images before brushing: a surface heatmap against one reported a "1 cm cheek gap" the real references did not confirm.
- **"I still see major differences" after a conform: locate the difference by layer before sculpting anything.** One cause per layer, each with a repeatable check; report which layer holds the difference, then ask which target the user means.
  1. *Our head against the approved 3D head*: signed surface gap by zone (forehead, brow ridge, eye area, nose, cheeks, mouth, chin, jaw sides, skull, ears; zones as boxes relative to pupil height and the midline). **Fit the target onto the exported head again on every run** (rigid ICP on face and skull, then a second table with size freed): a later body change moves the head off the stored alignment (1.6 cm once), and the stale alignment reported a 6 mm "gap" on a head that sat within 0.5 mm. Blind to a feature that slid along the skin and to occluded skin.
  2. *The approved 3D head against the pictures*: front landmarks on each reference set, the 3D head's textured render and a painted render of ours, as widths and pupil-line distances in pupil-distance units, each as % against one reference column. Include every reference set as its own column: their spread is the floor (4% on widths, 8% on eye opening here). Two more floors: our head and the 3D head read 4% apart on cheekbone width with surfaces under 1 mm apart (contour landmarks are guesses), and orthographic renders read outline widths several % wider than perspective pictures. Call anything inside those floors noise.
  3. *Everything that is not shape*: a ladder sheet adding one thing per panel (reference bald, 3D head in clay, ours in clay, ours with skin/eyes/brows, plus hair, reference haired), front and three-quarter rows, every panel placed by its own landmarks (eye midpoint fixed, eye-line-to-chin height fixed). The step where the look changes names the cause. That placement hides an overall long or short face; layer 2 carries it.
  - **Do not read a shape verdict off the clay panels.** They are orthographic and the pictures are perspective: flat-on, a jaw outline read 7% wider than the picture, and the 3D head looked harder and older (heavier brow ridge, squarer jaw). Offering "move the shape towards the pictures" on that read was wrong; the outline checks below then showed the shape already matched. Run them before naming any shape difference or offering a target decision:
    - *Outline width through a camera*: project the exported head's skin vertices through a pinhole in front of the pupils at several distances (60 cm to infinity), take silhouette width per row below the pupil line in projected pupil distances, and compare with the picture's figure mask at the same rows (0.8-1.6 pupil distances down: below the ears, above the shoulders). A match at a portrait distance (130-160 cm here, within 1%) means the width difference is the camera, not the head.
    - *Profile face line*: from the picture, the frontmost skin pixel per row (chroma mask `R - B > 25`, largest blob); from a head, the most forward skin point per 0.4 mm of height over vertices, edge middles and triangle centres. Align at the nasion, scale by nasion-to-chin, read depth per 0.5 cm in cm on our head, and include both profile pictures as columns (they differed by 2 mm on the nose). Find anchors by bands in crown-to-tip heights H below the tip: chin = most forward in 0.38-0.52 H (lower lip sits at 0.30, the groove at 0.36, the underside at 0.55), nasion = deepest within 0.40 H above. Draw them on the pictures and look. Read features, not single rows: on the steep underside of the nose a small height offset shows as a 3-5 mm depth "gap".
  - **Then show a fair sheet and ask what the user sees**: reference bald beside ours with the hair slot cleared, under the plugin's flat-light map (`LightingEnvironments/TextureBooth`, fixed exposure about EV100 -2.9 with the capture helper), same camera distance, each panel placed by its own eye line and chin, front and three-quarter. List the differences you see as numbered, by-eye items (brow height and arch, eye size, nose tip, mouth, lower face, skin) and ask which match theirs; measure only the ones they pick. That map lights from the front, so a 90-degree view is half dark and its skin mask mis-scales: leave the profile panel off and carry the profile as the geometry numbers.
  - When outline and profile are at the floor, the differences are form inside the outline and what sits on the skin. Say that the edits you proposed are withdrawn and why, before asking.
  - **Measure each item the user picks before describing its direction; the by-eye list is only a prompt.** Brows listed as "lower, close on the eyes" measured 3-4 mm *higher* than the pictures. Brow recipe: on each front image (references and a flat-light capture with brows on), take the luminance column over the pupil, 0.2 pupil distances towards the nose and 0.25 towards the ear; the brow is the darkest run 0.12-0.75 pupil distances above the pupil line, its edges at half contrast. Report low edge, top and thickness per station; MediaPipe's brow rows (`52/282` over lid `159/386`, `55/285` over corner `133/362`) should agree in sign. A haired reference fails at the inner and outer stations (the fringe is the darkest run): use the bald and buzz sets.
  - **A feature moved to its measured place can still look worse.** Brows lowered onto the pictures' numbers read more scowling over a lid more hooded than the picture's. Say so beside the sheet, keep the trial unsaved and name the neighbour (lid fold) as a possible next item, then take the user's verdict: they kept the measured position that the assistant's eye had doubted. Your by-eye doubt is a note for the user, never a reason to revert or retune before they answer.
  - Expect most picked items to come back inside the floor (eyes, nose and mouth widths, jaw, chin within 4%). Report them as "position and size match; the difference is surface form or colour", one line each, and do not invent an edit for them.
- **"The face is too hard / too old" is a shape-history, skin and brow question before it is a new sculpt.**
  1. Search the commit log and the edit scripts for an earlier pass that deliberately strengthened jaw, chin or cheekbones beyond the conform target. If one exists, taking it back is the first softening pass: subtract that pass's recorded displacement (positions before and after it) from the current head, faded to zero within ~2.6-3.6 cm of each pupil so eye and lid edits made in the same pass stay. Tell the user the earlier request and this one pull opposite ways.
  2. Expect that undo (1-3 mm) to read as a small change. Name what still carries the hardness and offer it next: tanned, red, glossy skin against a pale matte reference; thick dark brows on the eyes; brow ridge and mouth folds that belong to the approved 3D head.
  3. Preview the edited positions as clay beside the current ones with a moved-distance colour map before importing, then import isolated from the body and confirm the result equals the request (it did to 0.001 mm).
- **A feature the user says is missing (chin cleft, dimple): confirm it in every reference set on a landmark-centred crop, then measure ours on the mesh.** Groove depth = front-most skin on the midline minus the mean of the same 0.7 cm either side, per 0.5 cm of height. For a cleft, find the groove under the lower lip and the pad's front on the midline profile, then press a Gaussian groove (start at 1.3 mm deep, 0.28 cm half-width) along the vertex normals between them, ending 0.4 of the groove-to-pad distance below the pad's front. Depth and length are separate faults: 2 mm run as far below the pad's front as the lip groove is above was judged far too strong, mostly because it notched the chin's bottom outline; the same shortened groove at 0.8 mm was too shallow; 1.3 mm was accepted. When one verdict says too strong and the next too shallow, take the middle of the two and change nothing else. Say depth and width are by-eye, and show a chin crop with one panel per reference set and per depth tried, each labelled with its depth and verdict.
  - Recompute every depth from the same pre-edit export, kept under its own file name, and import it over the previous try. A target script run on its own output stacks the edit; give such a script an assert that its input still equals the pre-edit head.
- **"The cheeks look gaunt" has no picture measurement yet; say so and work by eye on a trial.** The far-cheek outline of a three-quarter picture against the head turned to the same angle is ruled by the turn, not the cheek: two estimates of one picture's turn (nose tip between the pupils, pupil spacing over pupils-to-chin height) gave 23 and 42 degrees, and the verdict flipped with them. Report the attempt as inconclusive rather than quoting either table.
  - Fill the hollow under the cheekbone (between mouth corner and ear, about 7 cm below the pupils, 5.5 cm from the midline), not the front of the cheek: a fill centred beside the nose moved the mouth corner and changed nothing visible.
  - Move the whole patch in one direction (the skin normal at its centre) with a radial falloff, hidden skin included. Per-vertex normals flip triangles in the folds inside the mouth corner, and so does cutting the falloff off at occluded skin.
  - Print what the fill moves at lips, nose wings, eye area and the widest point of the face before importing, and name what a fill cannot touch (nose-to-mouth creases, glossy tanned skin) as the likelier cause of an older read.
  - **Verify each ladder panel shows what its label says** with a pixel diff against its neighbour before sending. Clearing a brow slot left the hairs in the preview while hair cleared at once; two identical panels were caught only by the diff. Drop a panel you cannot make true and say so. The same goes for a "no hair" label on a trial capture: a hair slot cleared before a template import came back with hair on, so look at the capture before writing the caption.
  - Take the ladder's engine panels as an unsaved trial after any pending look decision is saved or reverted: the trial ends with a package reload, which would discard an unanswered change.
  - There is no single likeness number; say so when the user asks for a deterministic method. The layers say where a difference comes from, the user's eye says whether it matters.
- **A crease, line or shadow the user names: find it on a full-resolution crop before choosing what to change.** Crop about 0.3 pupil distances either side of the feature from the reference and from our capture at the same scale, and render the same area in clay. At sheet size a "crease above the nose" was taken for the shallow vertical valley between the brows; the full-size crop showed a sharp horizontal highlight across the root of the nose, and the first fix was thrown away with its rebuild wait.
  - Put a number on it from the mesh: front-most skin on the midline per 0.25 cm of height, and the second difference of that column. A ledge shows as a large bend followed by one of opposite sign (the nose root stepped forward 2 mm in a quarter centimetre; bend 1.8 mm). The conform target's own column tells whether the ledge came with the target.
  - **Never smooth a ledge at the nose root by pushing skin back.** Fitting the step onto an even curve moved the lower brow back up to 2.5 mm, which deepened the dip between nose and brow; the user then reported a sunken, shadowed divot. Only move skin forward there.
  - **A sunken nose-to-brow dip ("divot", "brow should flow into the nose"): fill it, then smooth.**
    1. Measure the dip on the midline only: front-most skin per 0.25 cm of height from the nose bridge (1.5 cm below the pupils) to the brow ridge (1.5 cm above), against the straight line joining those two ends. Beside the midline the same line spans the eye socket and asks for a 10 mm fill.
    2. Move the skin at each height forward by a fraction of that midline gap. Fade sideways to nothing by 1.6 cm (the inner eye corners sit near 1.9-2.0), and let the move grow to about 1.2 times the midline's at 0.9 cm out: the pit seen from the front is mostly the hollows beside the nose root.
    3. Then run a normal-only Laplacian smooth over the nose root and the brow ridge between the brows (each round: half way to the edge-neighbours' average, along the vertex normal, about 15 rounds). Weld vertices doubled at UV seams before building neighbours, or the seam opens. The user asked for this smoothing after the fill and accepted the result.
    - What was accepted here: a 7.5 mm dip filled to about 4 mm (0.3 of the gap, plus three rounds of lifting the midline to its own 0.35 cm running average), then the smooth. 0.3 of the gap alone was twice judged still too deep.
    - **Front and side views can disagree, and the user's front-view read decides.** Before any fill the midline profile matched the profile pictures within 1 mm, yet the user saw the dip as too deep from the front; the accepted fill leaves the profile several mm flatter than the pictures. Report the conflict with its number in every round and offer the sides-only variant, then build what they ask for.
    - Print the largest move on the midline, at 0.6-1.2 cm out and beyond 1.4 cm, and the eyeballs' nearest approach to the midline, before importing.
  - **Filling the nose root breaks every later profile measurement that aligns at the nasion** (the deepest point moves to the forehead; nasion-to-chin read 13.2 cm instead of 10.7). Before the first fill, copy the exported head under its own name and keep it as the profile anchor; measure nose and mouth on it for as long as they are unchanged, and assert that before trusting the table.
  - **Compare two heads only after fitting one onto the other.** A column-by-column read of our head against the stored conform target, without the refit, reported the flanks of the nose root 2-5 mm more sunken; the fitted surface gap for the same patch was within 0.3 mm.
  - When two features could be the one meant, fix the one the crop shows and name the other in the report as left alone.
- **"The brow is not quite right" is not a request to build yet.** Ask whether it is the hairs or the ridge under them, and which trait (angle, bushiness, colour, how close they start), with your own read of the reference as a prompt. A measured difference is not that answer: brows that started 0.31 pupil distances from the middle against the pictures' 0.22-0.24 were slid inward to 0.25 and read no better, at the cost of several groom rebuilds. Report the measurement and wait.
  - Brow start and end from a front image: threshold pixels darker than 0.72 of the locally blurred luminance in the band above the lids, per column; the first and last columns with a run over 0.02 pupil distances. It finds the dense body, not the sparse hairs at the nose end, so the visible start sits nearer the middle than the number.
  - Sliding a brow groom sideways along the groom head: re-project onto the skin while holding both the sideways and the height coordinate (the nearest skin point otherwise pulls hairs back and down where the brow curves round, and the tails droop), then drop hairs whose longest segment more than doubled (they landed half on the lid and break the colour-map paint's seam check).
- **"The nose needs work": the side-view line is the measurable part; move it towards the pictures' average.** Sample our midline and each profile picture per 0.05 cm from 1.9 cm below the nose root to 5.6 cm (just under the nose), all aligned and scaled as in the profile face line recipe, and move the skin forward or back by 0.8 of the smoothed gap to the pictures' mean. Full strength to 0.7 cm from the midline, gone by 1.9 so the wings and the front-view width stay (wings moved 0.2 mm). Here the bridge and tip came back up to 2.9 mm and the skin under the nose forward 1.9 mm, leaving under 1 mm against pictures that disagree with each other by about 2 mm. Print the per-height gap with the pictures' spread beside it, and rerun the profile table on anchor-head-plus-move to confirm.
  - From the front and three-quarter the change is subtle; say so, and get a usable profile capture or say there is none.
- **Do not move the upper lip back from a profile gap: the teeth do not move.** A 1.8 mm push put lip points behind the teeth surface (3 to 17 points). Check any lip move by counting lip points that sit just behind the nearest teeth vertex (teeth are their own material section) before and after, and stop the profile patch above the lip when the count rises. Report the lip gap as needing the teeth moved too.
- **"The mouth needs work": measure colour as well as shape before proposing.** Front widths and lip heights usually sit inside the floor. Sample upper lip, lower lip and the skin above and below the mouth as linear-light ratios to the cheek on each reference and on a flat-light capture: here the lower lip matched and the upper lip was much lighter than the references' (about 0.7 of cheek brightness against 0.5). Part of that is lighting on a downward-facing surface, so present it as the likely biggest gap and ask before repainting.
- **Render framing alone moves scores by up to 0.6.** Use one camera rig for every candidate, or the score differences are noise.
- **Hairless reference sets:** use a minimal-edit prompt (`replace only his hair... everything else pixel-identical... do not make him older/harder`) on each existing haired view, not new angles from a bald front.
  - A 1-2 mm buzz cut drifts less: about 0.6 front, about 1.0 at the 3/4 views.
  - Fully shaved reads harsher and older and drifts to 1.2-1.7.
  
  Score each edit against its haired original with the landmark tool.
  Once the user accepts a buzz set, it is the sculpt reference for forehead, temples, ears and skull; judge overall likeness and hair against the haired set.
- **Showing a generated 3D head to the user:** one sheet per candidate, rows = the input images, the textured render, and a flat grey clay render (geometry only), columns = front / both 3/4 / profile at the same yaws as the references. Clay exposes solid-mesh hair and shape drift that the texture hides. Post the sheets directly when asked to see results, then restate the open decisions.
