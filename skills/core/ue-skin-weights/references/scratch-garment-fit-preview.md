# Check garment cut before changing weights

1. For trousers built from a body distance field, intersect the actual body triangles with horizontal planes from the thighs into the seat. Measure the inner skin boundary on each leg at each height. Keep the garment’s inner surface within the available gap with room for the skin; taper the gap closed where the legs join. Uniform outward offsets bridge the legs, while a fixed-width carve can cut through skin. Skin-weight transfer fixes neither error.
2. Preserve the old cut as a control. In PIE, attach a transient `SkeletalMeshActor`’s movable, collision-disabled component at identity to the real player’s body and call `set_leader_pose_component` on the garment. Read back the leader and mesh. This tests skinning only; leave the player class, body and face unchanged.
3. Swap old and new garments on the same held pose and camera. Check the original failing pose first, then idle, both strides and the passing pose from front and back. Include hands and hems for a reach; crop the inseam separately if the full-body framing hides exposed skin. Report which views passed. A better lower-leg silhouette does not prove the standing defect is gone or that the fabric moves like wool.

## Separate fit at rest from motion-induced clipping

1. With cloth simulation off, track the **same garment vertex IDs** against the posed body at idle, lift, peak and release. At each ID measure `(vertex - nearest_body_point) · outward_body_normal`; use one penetration tolerance throughout. Split peak failures into IDs already inside at idle and IDs that cross only during motion. A fixed screen crop cannot track material points across poses.
2. For motion-only failures, test a local rebind first. Interpolate weights from the nearest idle body triangle, normalize the affected rows, and inverse-transform the original posed shirt points through the new blended idle transforms to preserve the idle silhouette. Compare original → rebind alone → rebind plus local clearance across the same poses; this separates the benefit of each edit.
3. Taper the remaining position correction into the surrounding panel, pin sewn openings, and check original versus revised triangle normals for reversals before export. Recheck on the player with unmasked skin. Report source-vertex counts separately from camera-depth pixels: a clean rendered patch does not certify the whole mesh.

## Restrict an underarm repair to the underside

1. Identify the sewn gusset from panel membership or its UV island. On a scratch adjacency graph, join known UV-split copies of the same sewn vertex, not separate layers that happen to touch. Weight triangle edges by rest length; run multi-source shortest paths from gusset vertices. Distance along the cloth keeps the correction from jumping across the armpit to the shoulder.
2. Fade the candidate’s rest-position and bone-weight deltas by that surface distance. Set the fade to zero on the accepted shoulder top and neckline. Prune and normalize only affected rows; assert `np.array_equal` for protected source positions and weights before export. Keep the geometry’s UV splits intact.
3. Compare the accepted and revised cuts with unchanged solver settings first. Check idle, elbow bend, lift, full reach and lowering; inspect both shoulder tops and both sides of the underarm. Use adjacent-face bend angles on the same interior edges to locate stubborn folds, not to approve the garment: a smoother reach can still expose skin at rest. Only then test a narrower simulated sleeve region on the same mesh.
